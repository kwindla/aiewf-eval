# Gemma 4 targeted KV-precision study

This directory contains the frozen corpus, direct replay harness, and analysis
artifacts for the local NVFP4-weight Gemma 4 31B BF16-versus-FP8 KV experiment.
The study tests turns 12 and 15 separately with byte-identical histories and
paired SGLang sampling seeds.

The balanced 300-history follow-up requested by the post-hoc provenance
finding is complete. See
`balanced-origin-followup-20260808/REPORT.md`. Its turn-12 BF16-minus-FP8
effects are +11.1 points on BF16-origin histories and +7.1 points on
FP8-origin histories; the +4.0-point origin interaction has a 95% interval of
-4.5 to +12.5 points and does not reproduce the earlier four-history sign
reversal.

## Final result

The preregistered maximum campaign completed on 2026-08-08. All 32,768 rows
passed per-block and cumulative integrity audits. At the cumulative
8,192-case-per-turn/arm look, both primary real-prefix-bank intervals met the
frozen +/-2 percentage-point precision target, so the machine stopping
decision was `stop_precision_met` and no additional sampling is required.

| Primary warm-cache cell | BF16 success | FP8 success | BF16-FP8 | Two-turn/three-look simultaneous interval |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Turn 12 real-prefix bank | 64.2% | 48.3% | +15.9 pp | +13.9 to +17.8 pp |
| Turn 15 real-prefix bank | 56.5% | 56.0% | +0.5 pp | -0.9 to +2.0 pp |

Within the preregistered mixed-prefix bank, this is precise evidence of a
direct, turn-specific interaction between conversational state and the tested
unit-scaled FP8 E4M3 KV path at turn 12. It is not an on-policy deployment
estimate or evidence of a history-independent BF16 advantage. The post-hoc
source split below reverses sign: BF16 wins on the eight BF16-based histories,
while FP8 wins on average on the four FP8-origin histories. The pooled
+15.9-point magnitude therefore depends materially on the bank's history mix.

The corresponding turn-15 conditional effect is small: the adjusted interval
includes zero and bounds effects compatible with these data to approximately
-0.9 to +2.0 points. None of this establishes that every FP8 KV implementation
has the same behavior. The checkpoint exposes no KV-cache scale scheme on this
pinned SGLang path, so the FP8 arm uses K/V scales of 1.0. Calibration remains
a separate mechanism experiment.

The turn-12 effect is heterogeneous across the frozen histories: seven of the
12 prefixes favor BF16, four favor FP8, and one ties, with large differences in
both directions. The precise primary result is the preregistered
equal-prefix-weighted bank average, not a claim that BF16 wins for every prompt
state or that FP8 causes a uniform loss of model capability.

The estimand is conditional on the frozen 12-prefix bank, normal warm-prefix
reuse, the matched compact server geometry, and the retained seed-only sampler
shim. The independent 150-conversation arms remain the production evidence:
BF16 was +0.67 points overall, with a whole-conversation bootstrap 95% interval
of -0.07 to +1.40 points, and had 70 rather than 98 turn-12 errors. That
on-policy comparison includes any earlier-history mediation and does not
isolate the instantaneous KV effect. Historical mediation analysis also found
a plausible upstream-history pathway for turn 15, while the warm/cold pilot
was too small to establish cache-path equivalence. These findings answer
different questions and should not be collapsed into one estimate.

## Turn 12 error-pattern analysis

This descriptive analysis follows the sealed confirmatory result and does not
alter its estimand or interval. Its machine-readable counts, all-prefix table,
and representative exact outputs are in
`results/v2-turn12-error-pattern-analysis.{json,md}` and are regenerated by
`analyze_turn12_error_patterns.py`.

### Aggregate failure shift

| Outcome | BF16 | FP8 | FP8 - BF16 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Correct tool and arguments | 3,943/6,144 (64.18%) | 2,967/6,144 (48.29%) | -15.89 pp |
| Redundant confirmation or question | 814/6,144 (13.25%) | 1,552/6,144 (25.26%) | **+12.01 pp** |
| False claim of completion | 1,326/6,144 (21.58%) | 1,473/6,144 (23.97%) | +2.39 pp |
| Other no-tool response | 54/6,144 (0.88%) | 149/6,144 (2.43%) | **+1.55 pp** |

The additional 738 redundant-question labels numerically account for 75.6%
of the 976-call success deficit. Wrong tools, wrong arguments, malformed calls,
and request failures are negligible. The shift is overwhelmingly from the
required structured call into ordinary assistant prose.

### The dominant prefix property is provenance

The direct replay intentionally sends each frozen history through both KV
arms. This controls the input state and isolates what changes at that state,
but one arm necessarily receives an off-policy history. The post-hoc source
split shows that this matters:

| Prefix source | Prefixes | BF16 success | FP8 success | BF16 - FP8 | FP8 - BF16 redundant |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| BaseTen BF16 | 4 | 78.7% | 49.3% | +29.4 pp | +16.0 pp |
| Local BF16 | 4 | 65.4% | 37.5% | +27.9 pp | +18.7 pp |
| Local FP8 | 4 | 48.4% | 58.1% | **-9.7 pp** | +1.4 pp |

Across the eight BF16-based histories, BF16 wins by 28.7 points. Across the
four local-FP8 histories, FP8 wins by 9.7 points. Because the frozen bank has
twice as many BF16-based as FP8-origin histories, the +15.9-point pooled effect
must not be presented as the expected real-world BF16-versus-FP8 difference.
It is the valid answer to the narrower preregistered question: the average
instantaneous effect over this particular mixed bank.

The sign reversal is compatible with a history-manifold or on-policy
compatibility effect, in which each inference path is more stable on histories
it helped produce. It does not prove that mechanism. There are only four
selected histories per source, and source is confounded with wording,
deployment, and snapshot selection. In particular, the BaseTen histories came
from BF16 weights/KV plus MTP, not the otherwise matched local NVFP4 deployment.
A clean direct-effect follow-up should use a much larger, balanced random sample
of local BF16- and local FP8-origin histories, report the effect within each
source stratum, and make the source-by-inference interaction a primary outcome.
For the production-level total effect, the existing full-conversation campaigns
in which each arm retains its own history are the appropriate evidence.

### What the high-shift prefixes have in common

Four prefixes produce 666 of the net 738 extra redundant labels (90.2%):

| Prefix | Origin | FP8 - BF16 redundant | FP8 - BF16 other no-tool | BF16 - FP8 success |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `turn12-local_fp8-02` | Local FP8 | +40.6 pp | +3.9 pp | +60.4 pp |
| `turn12-local_bf16-03` | Local BF16 | +33.4 pp | +3.3 pp | +57.6 pp |
| `turn12-baseten_bf16-01` | BaseTen BF16 | +29.9 pp | +5.5 pp | +67.6 pp |
| `turn12-baseten_bf16-02` | BaseTen BF16 | +26.2 pp | +2.3 pp | +61.1 pp |

The concentration is not a late-seed artifact. Nine of 12 prefixes have a
positive redundant-label shift in every 128-seed quartile, and the bank-level
increase is +12.2, +12.5, +11.5, and +11.8 points across those quartiles. Three
prefixes run strongly in the other direction, however: `baseten_bf16-04`
(-14.5 points), `local_fp8-01` (-23.0), and `local_fp8-03` (-23.2).

Simple missing-state and length explanations do not fit. All 12 snapshots:

- contain identical system instructions, tools, and user utterances;
- already know the name Jennifer Smith;
- immediately precede the target with a correct call of the same tool using
  that name, followed by a success result;
- receive the same complete second suggestion; and
- differ by only 95 prompt tokens, from 13,956 to 14,051.

After generated call IDs are removed, the final four messages are exactly
identical. The target is nevertheless a delicate state-machine transition. It
is an anaphoric repeat action ("one more suggestion") phrased as a soft
question ("How about ...?"). A generic conversational policy can treat that as
an invitation to acknowledge or reconfirm, even though benchmark state says to
reuse the established name and call the tool immediately.

Earlier assistant wording is the remaining observable variation. The four
histories whose name acknowledgement begins `Nice to meet you, Jennifer!`
move from 4.3% BF16 redundant to 34.1% FP8 redundant (+29.8 points), compared
with +3.1 points for the other eight. This is hypothesis-generating only. One
non-`Nice to meet you` prefix also has a +33.4-point jump, and the histories
differ at many earlier assistant turns; a controlled wording-swap probe would
be needed to attribute causality to that phrase or style.

### Redundant confirmation is primarily a slot-persistence failure

Paired seeds show 1,031 correct-BF16 responses becoming FP8 redundant questions,
versus 472 transitions in the other direction: a net +559. Another net +176
comes from false-completion responses changing into redundant questions. Thus
75.7% of the redundant-category increase is a direct imbalance at the
correct-call/redundant-question boundary, while 23.8% is a change in the style
of an already incorrect no-tool response.

The post-hoc subtype audit makes the semantic pattern clearer:

| Redundant-question subtype | BF16 | FP8 | FP8 - BF16 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Reconfirm identity or ownership | 424 | 888 | **+464** |
| Reconfirm content or intent | 263 | 429 | +166 |
| Reconfirm authorization | 42 | 68 | +26 |
| Claim completion, then ask a follow-up | 44 | 82 | +38 |
| Other question | 41 | 85 | +44 |

Identity or ownership reconfirmation supplies 62.9% of the net category
increase. Typical failures explicitly remember Jennifer and then ask whether
the second suggestion should also use her name. This is not simple retrieval
loss. It is failure to carry an already filled `name` slot across two
consecutive uses of the same action.

The benchmark prompt says models **must** collect required tool parameters but
only **should** use information already known. That asymmetry plausibly leaves
room for an over-cautious model to re-collect a value. A future benchmark
variant could state that previously supplied values count as collected and
must not be reconfirmed unless the user changes them or creates a genuine
ambiguity. That would be a benchmark change and should be evaluated separately,
not retroactively applied to these results.

### The 2.8x increase in other no-tool responses

`no_tool_other` rises from 54 to 149 cases, or 0.88% to 2.43%. It is a residual
mechanical category rather than a coherent behavior:

| Other no-tool subtype | BF16 | FP8 | FP8 - BF16 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Promise a future action without calling | 31 | 77 | **+46** |
| Print a textual pseudo-tool call | 13 | 52 | **+39** |
| False-completion wording missed by the frozen regex | 10 | 20 | +10 |

The first two subtypes account for 89.5% of the net increase. Four prefixes
account for +89 of the net +95 cases. Paired seeds contain 116 correct-BF16 to
FP8-other transitions but only 31 in reverse, a net +85. In the pseudo-call
cases, the response often prints the correct function name and arguments as
text while emitting an empty structured `tool_calls` field. Again, the model
usually has the task content; it fails at action-channel selection.

### A tool-channel decision boundary

The teacher-forced probe compares the expected first `<|tool_call>` token with
the best ordinary-assistant alternative (`I` or the assistant-channel token).
Across the 12 prefixes, the BF16-minus-FP8 change in that margin correlates
strongly with the observed BF16-minus-FP8 success change (Pearson r=0.931;
Spearman rho=0.909). Its post-hoc Pearson association with the redundant-label
change is r=0.810.

Together with the preserved names, topics, function names, and arguments, this
supports a general working model: these failures occur at the early branch
between structured action and fluent prose. Once the prose branch wins, the
model settles into familiar helpful-assistant modes—confirmation, promise,
narration, or an unsupported completion claim. The evidence localizes the
behavioral boundary; it does not identify a layer, attention head, or universal
FP8 mechanism.

This matters beyond the quantization study. Redundant confirmation is a
recurrent, high-leverage error on AIEWF's tool-commitment turns and dominates
the residual errors of several otherwise capable configurations. The broad
lesson is not that every strong model or every FP8 cache fails this way. It is
that repeated-action turns with inherited slots expose a narrow
tool-call-versus-prose boundary that aggregate pass rates can hide.

The frozen scorer itself is deliberately broad: after checking a narrower
false-completion pattern, any no-tool response with confirmation language or a
question mark receives the redundant label. The aggregate labels are therefore
mechanical, not independently human-adjudicated semantic categories. The
subtype analysis is post-hoc and descriptive.

Current gate status:

- 26 request snapshots frozen: 24 real prefixes and two golden mechanism probes.
- Seed allocation and FP8/BF16/BF16/FP8 macro schedule frozen.
- Mechanical scorer validation passed: 897/900 raw agreement (99.667%); all
  three disagreements are understood early-call realignment cases.
- Historical mediation screen complete. Turn-14 use of Jennifer's name strongly
  predicts turn-15 success and differs between the historical KV arms, so an
  upstream-history pathway is plausible and must be reported alongside the
  frozen-prefix direct effect.
- Historical wire audit found that Pipecat ignored configured `top_k=64`; replay
  correctly omits top-k.
- The FP8 compact server completed a normal 30-turn conversation.
- Prompt token IDs match across BF16 and FP8 for all 26 snapshots.
- The 512-token cap is cleared in both arms: 128/128 seeded comparisons per
  arm matched the historical 8,192-token cap exactly, with no truncations.
- SGLang's broad deterministic mode is unsuitable here: it disables insertion
  of completed requests into the radix cache and stalled on the 14K-token Gemma
  request. A narrow, retained shim instead makes the PyTorch sampler honor the
  request seed without changing model kernels or cache policy.
- Seed-only warm/warm and cold/cold smoke requests reproduce semantically in
  both KV arms. SGLang's generated tool-call UUID is transport metadata and is
  intentionally excluded from the repeatability signature.
- The first 924-request FP8 warm pilot extension passed a retrospective,
  fail-closed audit: exact allocation and reconstructed request/event hashes,
  fresh rescoring, verified cache ratios, and timestamps bound to the validated
  FP8 container instance. Its sidecar is
  `results/fp8-cache-pilot-warm-retrospective-sidecar.json`.
- An independent pre-primary audit paused collection until treatment identity,
  resume invariants, allocation completeness, parser/truncation checks, and
  seed-cluster inference were hardened. All later stages used immutable v2
  collection plans. The restart-separated repeatability gates and all planned
  cache-pilot cells completed; warm/cold effect equivalence remained

- Warm-versus-cold output differences occur in both KV arms and are analyzed as

Each output gets a sibling `.plan.json` and `.audit.json`. A plan binds the
requested label to the exact live Docker instance, checkpoint/image, KV dtype,
server command/environment, seed shim, source hashes, allocation, and request
hashes. Resume aborts if any invariant differs. A malformed tool parse,
truncation, request error, or cache-gate failure is retained and stops the stage.
